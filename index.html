Business Question

Which customer segments drive revenue, and where is revenue at risk?

Approach

  • Data Cleaning (Python): Removed cancelled orders, returns, non-positive quantities and prices, duplicates and missing customer IDs — 1,067,371 raw rows → 779,425 clean rows.
  • RFM Segmentation: Scored each customer 1–5 on Recency, Frequency and Monetary value. Segmented into Champions, Loyal, At Risk, New, Lost, Others.
  • Cohort Retention: Grouped customers by first-purchase month and tracked month-over-month retention.
  • Power BI Dashboard: Built 5 visuals — KPI cards, revenue by segment, customer count by segment, revenue over time and a cohort retention heatmap.

Key Findings

Champions drive 68% of revenue

Despite being only 22% of customers — extreme revenue concentration.

£1.09M in At-Risk revenue

353 high-value customers (£3,089 average each) showing declining engagement.

Retention is declining in newer cohorts

Month-1 retention fell from 35% (Dec 2009) to 17% (mid-2010) — acquisition quality is deteriorating.

Recommendations

  1. Protect Champions — introduce loyalty tiering, early access and dedicated account management for the 1,297 customers driving 68% of revenue.
  2. Reactivate At-Risk segment — targeted win-back campaign for the 353 customers worth £1.09M.
  3. Fix onboarding — Month-1 retention dropped 40% in newer cohorts; review acquisition targeting and first-purchase experience.

Task 1: Natural Gas Price Forecasting

📌 Problem:

The trading desk wanted to start trading natural gas storage contracts but lacked a way to forecast future prices. The existing data was just monthly snapshots, with no model accounting for seasonal trends (higher winter demand = higher prices). They needed price estimates for any date to price contracts accurately.

✅ Solution:

Built a linear regression model with two components: a linear trend (upward over time) and monthly seasonal dummies (to capture winter/summer price differences). The model achieved an out-of-sample MAE of ~$0.17/MMBtu and RMSE of ~0.20. Delivered a Python function that estimates the price on any date and provides a 12‑month forecast.

Task 2: Commodity Storage Contract Pricing

📌 Problem:

A client wanted to buy gas in summer, store it, and sell it in winter to profit from seasonal price differences. However, they needed a pricing model that accounted for multiple injection/withdrawal dates, storage capacity limits, daily injection/withdrawal rates, and all associated costs (storage rental, injection fees, withdrawal fees). Without this, they couldn't determine if the trade was actually profitable.

✅ Solution:

Developed a Python pricing function that simulates the full lifecycle of a storage contract. The function:

  • Accepts injection/withdrawal dates, volumes, rates, max capacity, and cost inputs.
  • Simulates inventory levels and enforces daily rate constraints.
  • Calculates gross profit from price differences and subtracts all storage, injection, and withdrawal costs.
  • Returns the net contract value, enabling traders to evaluate profitability instantly.
Download Solution (PDF)

Task 3: Credit Risk & Expected Loss Prediction

📌 Problem:

The retail banking arm was experiencing higher-than-expected default rates. They needed a model to estimate the Probability of Default (PD) for each borrower and calculate the Expected Loss (EL) so they could set aside sufficient capital reserves. They lacked a predictive tool that could analyze customer characteristics (income, FICO score, debt-to-income ratio, etc.) to flag at-risk borrowers.

Solution:

Built a Random Forest classifier using features like credit_lines_outstanding, loan_amt_outstanding, total_debt_outstanding, income, years_employed, fico_score, debt_to_income, and loan_to_income. The model achieves an AUC of ~0.75–0.80, accurately distinguishing between defaulters and non-defaulters. Combined with a 10% recovery rate, I created a function that computes Expected Loss (EL) = PD × (1 − Recovery Rate) × Loan Amount, giving the bank a concrete dollar value for potential losses.

Business Problem

Geldium, a financial services client, faced rising credit card delinquency rates and high operational costs from manual, reactive outreach. The goal was to use predictive analytics to identify at-risk customers early and automate personalized interventions to prevent delinquency while maintaining fairness and regulatory compliance.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

>50% Credit Utilization

Strongest predictor of delinquency

3+ Missed Payments

High-risk flag within 6 months

DTI > 0.5

Debt-to-income ratio spike

Anomaly Detected

High income / low credit score – flagged for review

📌 Missing income and loan balance values were handled via median imputation.

Predictive Model – Logistic Regression

  • Why: Highly interpretable – essential for financial services transparency and stakeholder trust.
  • Key Features: Credit Utilization, Missed Payments, Income, Debt-to-Income Ratio, Account Tenure.
  • Evaluation Metrics: Precision (avoid false alarms), Recall (catch high-risk customers), F1-Score (balance), AUC (class separation).
  • Bias & Fairness: Evaluated across age and income groups – no disproportionate flagging observed.

System Architecture

Data Pipeline → ⚙️ Decision Engine → 📨 Action Layer → Learning Loop
  • Data Pipeline: Ingests real-time customer financial behaviour.
  • Decision Engine: ML models + business rules → optimal intervention.
  • Action Layer: Automated SMS/email, repayment plans, hardship offers.
  • Learning Loop: Continuously improves based on repayment outcomes.

Strategic Recommendation

Target Segment: Under 30 + 2+ Missed Payments

Launch a 6‑week pilot outreach campaign via SMS + Email offering flexible payment plans and financial counseling.

✅ Specific 📊 Measurable: 10‑15% ↓ ⚡ Actionable 🎯 Relevant ⏳ Time-bound (6 weeks)

Responsible AI Guardrails

  • Fairness: Regular bias audits – equal treatment across demographics.
  • Transparency: Clear explanations for every AI decision.
  • Compliance: Aligned with GDPR, ECOA, consumer laws.
  • Oversight: Human intervention for critical decisions.

Expected Business Impact

  • 15% reduction in 30+ day delinquency (6 months).
  • 60% of outreach actions automated – significant cost savings.
  • Scalable: AI allows mass personalisation without increasing headcount.
  • Trust: Fair, transparent outreach improves customer repayment rates.
